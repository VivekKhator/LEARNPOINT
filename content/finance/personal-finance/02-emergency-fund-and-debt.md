:::warning
Educational material, not advice.
:::

Before any investing question is worth discussing, two things need to be in place: a buffer against surprises, and the absence of debt that compounds faster than any investment can.

## The emergency fund

An emergency fund exists so that an unexpected expense does not become an expensive loan or a forced sale of long-term investments at the worst moment.

### Size it from expenses, not income

```text
monthly essentials = rent/EMI + utilities + groceries + transport
                   + insurance premiums + school fees + minimum debt payments
```

Essentials only. Not holidays, not subscriptions, not restaurants — the things you would still pay in a bad month.

| Situation | Months of essentials |
|---|---|
| Salaried, stable sector, dual income | 3–4 |
| Salaried, single income, dependants | 6 |
| Variable income — freelance, commission, business | 9–12 |
| Specialised role, long job search if displaced | 9–12 |

:::tip
Sizing on *income* rather than expenses typically inflates the target by 30–50%, parking a large sum in a low-return asset for years. Use the honest essentials number.
:::

### Where to keep it

Three properties, in priority order: **available within 24–48 hours**, **nominally stable**, and only then **earning something**.

| Place | Access | Suitability |
|---|---|---|
| Savings account | Instant | First 1 month — keep it boring |
| Sweep-in FD | Same day | Good for the bulk |
| Liquid / overnight fund | T+1 | Good, slightly better yield |
| Short-duration debt fund | T+1 | Acceptable; small price risk |
| Equity or equity funds | T+2 | **No** — can be down 30% precisely when you need it |
| Locked deposits, PPF, real estate | Weeks or years | **No** |

The point is not yield. Optimising an emergency fund for an extra 1% is optimising the wrong thing by an order of magnitude.

## Debt

### Not all debt is equivalent

| Debt | Typical rate | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Credit card revolve | 36–48% | Clear immediately. Nothing beats this return. |
| Personal loan | 11–20% | Expensive; clear early |
| Car loan | 9–12% | Depreciating asset — avoid borrowing long |
| Education loan | 8–12% | Can raise earning power; tax relief in some regimes |
| Home loan | 8–9.5% | Appreciating asset, tax relief, long tenure |

A home loan at 8.5% and a credit card at 42% are not the same instrument wearing different clothes. Treat them differently.

:::danger
Credit card minimum payments are designed to keep you borrowing. Paying the minimum on ₹1,00,000 at 42% takes well over a decade and costs more in interest than the original balance. "Minimum due" is the card issuer's business model, not a payment plan.
:::

### Two repayment orders

**Avalanche** — highest interest rate first. Mathematically optimal; costs the least in total interest.

**Snowball** — smallest balance first. Costs more, but clears individual debts quickly, and the visible progress keeps more people going to the end.

```text
Debts: ₹20,000 at 14%   |   ₹80,000 at 36%   |   ₹40,000 at 11%

Avalanche: 36% → 14% → 11%     (cheapest)
Snowball:  ₹20k → ₹40k → ₹80k  (fastest visible wins)
```

The difference in total interest is usually a few thousand rupees. The difference between a plan you finish and one you abandon is the whole balance. Choose accordingly, honestly.

## The ordering that works for most people

1. **Minimum payments on everything.** Defaults damage credit scores for years.
2. **A starter buffer — ₹25,000 to one month of expenses.** Stops the next surprise going back on the card.
3. **Clear debt above ~15%.** A 36% card is a guaranteed, tax-free 36% return. No investment matches that with certainty.
4. **Employer retirement match, if offered.** An instant 50–100% on the matched portion.
5. **Build the full emergency fund.** Three to six months.
6. **Clear debt in the 10–15% band.**
7. **Invest for long-term goals.**
8. **Prepay low-rate debt** — only if the certainty is worth more to you than the expected investment return.

Steps 3 and 4 sometimes swap. An employer match is typically a larger immediate return than even card interest, but only on the matched amount.

## A note on insurance

An emergency fund covers a lost month, a repair, a medical bill. It does not cover a catastrophe. Term life insurance (if anyone depends on your income) and health insurance are what stop a single event from wiping out a decade of saving.

:::warning
Term insurance and investment should be bought separately. Bundled endowment and ULIP products generally deliver less cover and worse returns than buying pure term cover and investing the difference — while being considerably harder to compare.
:::

## What to remember

- Size the fund on essential expenses, not income.
- An emergency fund is insurance — liquid and boring, never optimised for yield.
- High-interest debt is a guaranteed return no investment can match.
- Avalanche is cheaper; snowball finishes more often. Pick the one you will complete.
- Insure against catastrophe separately from investing.
